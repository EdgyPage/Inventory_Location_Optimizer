# S05 -- O3: the balance pools' SKU-run boundary in numpy; S06 -- O9: the fill-curve memo

## S05 -- O3 (decision c, the user's, 2026-09-24)

### Question

`_TravelBalancedPool` (rank_labor, rank_cartlabor) re-scored every live aisle at every SKU-
run boundary with one Python `_aisle_best` call each -- 72% of a campaign-shaped pool open,
and nearly every unit opens a run.  `_MinLaborPool` (rank_minlabor, rank_maxlabor) computed
every aisle's best-bracket cost and SORTED them at the boundary, then walked that order
per take in Python until an affinity prune that, on a `uni_` warehouse, rarely fires
(placement-sortmatch S09 measured the walk at 75-95% of the live aisles).  An exact early
stop was refuted (S09); the scan is inherent to both rules.  So: keep the scan, move it
into C.

### Prediction (registered in the plan)

Pool opens >= 3x faster (`Tests/bench/bench_pool_open.py`, campaign shape); `_aisle_best`
share of an open < 20%.

### What was built (`Warehouse/placement/Assignment_Functions.py`)

* `_TravelVec`: the aisles' bucket heads as a matrix (row = aisle in `by_aisle` order, the
  heap's rank; column = the aisle's own bucket order), built ONCE per pool.  A boundary is
  `PP[Mi] + Dh`, `argmin(axis=1)`, `load + fq*best` (+ the cart hinge); a take is `argmin`
  over the score vector; only the winner's row and score move.
* `_MinLabVec`: the bracket-end matrix (the representative end: near minimising, far
  maximising), the run's cost vector, `argsort(key, kind='stable')` for the order, the
  run's affinity deltas as a vector, and the walk as arrays -- a running min (max) gives the
  exact break the loop took, the first extreme before it the winner.
* Every clause of float and tie identity is argued in the two classes' docstrings (one
  IEEE addition; first-occurrence argmin = strict-`<` scan = heap `(score, rank)`; stable
  argsort = sorted `(key, rank, aid)`; the prune as a running extreme).
* Test re-pins, each with its reason: `test_placement_selection_is_not_a_scan.py` (zero
  `_aisle_best` calls at a boundary, one matrix build per pool, one vector boundary per
  run), `test_frozen_tier.py` (the walk order IS the stable argsort after every take, and
  scrambling it moves a take -- the old "a broken list raises" had no list left to break),
  `test_minlabor_pool_equivalence.py` (the CSR-order fold ratchet follows the fold into
  `_MinLabVec.set_delta`).

### Measurement

Oracles: `test_travel_balanced_equivalence`, `test_minlabor_pool_equivalence`,
`test_frozen_tier` (eager vs sliced, float equality, planted ties), the gain-plan and COW
suites, the pool audit -- **248 passed**, take sequences and scores IDENTICAL.

`bench_pool_open.py`, campaign shape (25,200 bins, 1,400 aisles, 12 units), median of 9,
measured while run A's parent was busy (both sides under the same load):

| open | before (de605170) | after | speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| travel, template (the gain evaluator's path) | 16.6 ms | **3.6 ms** | **4.6x** |
| minlabor, template | 28.5 ms | **5.9 ms** | **4.8x** |
| travel, eager (the put drain's path) | 21.8 ms | 8.2 ms | 2.6x |
| minlabor, eager | 31.9 ms | 10.5 ms | 3.0x |

**Prediction met** (>= 3x on the template path, 2.6-3.0x eager).  What is left of an eager
open is the pool's own construction over ~22k candidate bins, which tmin pays too (9 ms).

## S06 -- O9: the fill curve's per-(group, grid day) memo

### Question (found in S00)

35% of a single-cell 400k run's wall is parent setup, and ~10 of its ~19 minutes are the
stamped expected fill and its twelve-point transit curve, per channel.  One
`coverage.fill_rate` over the 240k-SKU store section is 10.8 s, 88% of it the compound-
Poisson recursion in `_served_under_lead` (`assets/s06_fill_profile.py`).

### Derivation

A curve point prices the SAME groups (shelves fixed) under a different transit law; a
group's per-grid-day sum depends on its shelves, rates, supplier leads, line laws and the
day `k`, never on the law's weight `pk`.  At the reference lead the twelve points reach
1,887 (point, day) pairs over only 534 distinct days -- a 3.5x ceiling.  Memoise the sum
per (group digest, k); each law re-weights it in its own order -- the same expression, the
same accumulation.

### Prediction (registered here before the real-data check)

Bit-identical stamp and curve; >= 2.5x on the curve's wall.

### Measurement (`assets/s06_curve_check.py`, 400k reference catalogue, store)

| | unmemoised | memoised |
|---|---|---|
| stamp + 12 curve points | 357.9 s | **108.9 s (3.3x)** |
| every float | -- | **bit-identical** |

`Tests/unit/test_fill_curve_memo.py` (2): memo == plain, bit for bit, and fewer passes;
a re-declared section reusing the memo equals a fresh memo (a moved shelf misses).

Expected saving per 400k run: ~4 min store + ~4 min fulfillment of the ~19-minute freeze.

### Not done (and why)

The line-floor solve (17 evaluations, ~3.4 min per channel) changes the shelves between
evaluations, so the group memo misses; a per-SKU memo would reuse most rows but relies on
`einsum`'s per-row reduction being independent of the group's composition -- plausible,
unproven, and a solved floor that moves by one step would move every level.  Left open.
