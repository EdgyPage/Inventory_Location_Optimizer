## 4. Staffing record

`Optimization/simconfig/staffing.py:20-21`, `:710-712`: put and receiving crews are **already** one site crew each, summed over both channels' per-day loads; `:723-727` already refuses channels with different batch counts because one batch is one site day. The record is the one layer that already models the coupled site.

What a leaf consumes today: `workunits.py:366-369` reads `derived.put.crew` / `derived.receiving.crew` and hands each leaf the **whole** site size as `put_crew_spec(size=...)` / `recv_crew_spec(size=...)`; `strategy_runner.py:541-581` (`_check_declared_crew`) verifies the payload matches the record. That is the double count: two independent processes each field the full site put and receiving crew, so the simulated site runs twice the derived labour. Coupling fixes this by construction (one dock and one put-crew allocator per process), and `expected_utilization`'s "single-channel leaves undercut rho" caveat (`staffing.py:663-667`, `equilibrium.py:37-38`) stops being needed for put and receiving. `channel_crew` / `picker_key` (`:354-385`) stay per channel (pickers really are per channel).

## Size call

**A redesign of the leaf model, not a handful of seams.** Three of the four layers change at their central abstraction: (a) the leaf identity itself -- `uid = (pair, config, channel, strategy)` (`workunits.py:902`), the `<channel>` directory level (`schema.py:71`) and the `sim_meta`-per-channel completeness marker (`runlayout.py:133`) all encode "one channel = one run"; (b) the receiving drain is a ~220-line manager method (`inventory_reorder.py:706-944`) reaching `_originals`, the packer and the per-unit ledger, which must move above two managers with owner routing at `_release_to_stock` (`:442`); (c) `run_channel_rollup.py`'s additivity premise and the yard evaluation scope are invalidated, not adapted. Two things are cheap: the transit/dock/packer/timeline seams are already injection (`Inventory_Management.py:254`, `:275`; `strategy_runner.py:1045`, `:1076`, `:1111`), so one object can be handed to two managers without touching `Warehouse/`; and `staffing.py` already derives site totals, so the coupled run is what the record has been describing all along. Expect the work to concentrate in a new site-level receiving/put-away coordinator plus a new work-unit and run-tree shape, with `_gain_bundle_for` (`strategy_runner.py:200`) the sharpest open design question: it must price two arms' placements from one trailer.
